About Dr. Rosenthal

A globally revered clinician-scientist who established benchmark paradigms in pediatric hepatobiliary diseases and liver transplantation.

Dr. Philip Rosenthal, MD, is an internationally celebrated pediatric hepatologist, clinical investigator, and Professor Emeritus of Pediatrics and Surgery at the University of California, San Francisco (UCSF). Throughout a clinical career spanning more than four decades, Dr. Rosenthal served as Director of Pediatric Hepatology and Medical Director of the Pediatric Liver Transplant Program at UCSF Benioff Children’s Hospital. His investigative inquiries and clinical innovations have fundamentally refined international diagnostic standards and therapeutic protocols for infants, children, and adolescents suffering from neonatal cholestatic jaundice, biliary atresia, progressive familial intrahepatic cholestasis, and fulminant acute hepatic failure.

A prolific investigator with more than 300 peer-reviewed scientific publications, Dr. Rosenthal has directed landmark multicenter NIH-funded consortia—including the Studies of Pediatric Liver Transplantation (SPLIT) and the Pediatric Acute Liver Failure (PALF) Study Group. He pioneered groundbreaking clinical trials with direct-acting antivirals (DAAs) in pediatric chronic hepatitis B and C, evaluated novel immunosuppressive strategies to preserve linear growth velocity while eliminating late allograft loss, and led consensus committees for the American Association for the Study of Liver Diseases (AASLD) and the North American Society for Pediatric Gastroenterology, Hepatology and Nutrition (NASPGHAN). Ask Dr. Philip Rosenthal encapsulates this immense academic corpus, offering fellows, attending physicians, and researchers immediate conversational access to four decades of peer-reviewed clinical knowledge.

What are the current management strategies and long-term outcomes for pediatric patients undergoing liver transplantation for biliary atresia?

Based on Dr. Philip Rosenthal’s longitudinal clinical cohorts at UCSF Benioff Children's Hospital and his steering contributions to the Studies of Pediatric Liver Transplantation (SPLIT) consortium:

  • Surgical Timing & Kasai Failure: Biliary atresia represents the indication for approximately 40%–50% of all pediatric liver transplants. While initial Kasai portoenterostomy delays cirrhosis in responders, recurrent cholangitis, intractable pruritus, and portal hypertension mandate early listing before severe failure to thrive and metabolic compromise intervene [Ref 1].
  • Long-Term Survival Metrics: Analysis of UCSF and multi-center SPLIT registry cohorts demonstrates 1-year and 10-year pediatric patient survival exceeding 92% and 86% respectively. Biliary atresia exhibits one of the most favorable long-term survival trajectories among all pediatric solid organ transplant indications [Ref 2].
  • Immunosuppression & Growth Preservation: Dr. Rosenthal’s clinical trials pioneered calcineurin inhibitor minimization combined with early steroid discontinuation (within 3–6 months post-transplant). This strategy significantly mitigates linear growth stunting, dyslipidemia, and osteopenia without increasing the incidence of acute cellular rejection [Ref 1, Ref 3].
  • Adolescent Transition & Chronic Graft Health: Current management strategies emphasize surveillance for chronic allograft arteriopathy, antibody-mediated rejection, and protocolized transition programs to counter non-adherence during adolescence [Ref 3].

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Ask Dr. Philip Rosenthal?

Ask Dr. Philip Rosenthal is an AI-powered academic and clinical research portal trained and grounded exclusively in Dr. Philip Rosenthal's extensive corpus of published research, clinical trials, textbook chapters, and medical guideline contributions across his 40+ year career at UCSF. It allows clinicians, researchers, fellows, and students to query decades of pediatric hepatology knowledge conversationally with immediate citations to the published literature.

Does this tool provide personalized medical advice or clinical diagnosis?

No. This tool is designed strictly as an informational and academic research assistant for healthcare professionals, clinical investigators, and trainees. It does not provide individualized medical diagnoses, clinical treatment plans, or formal medical care. Patients, guardians, and medical providers must consult attending physicians and qualified specialists for individual patient care decisions.

What source materials and data ground this AI tool?

The system indexes Dr. Rosenthal’s complete scientific publication record comprising over 300 peer-reviewed articles, multi-center NIH trials (such as the Pediatric Acute Liver Failure study group and the SPLIT consortium), landmark clinical trials in pediatric hepatitis B and C, medical society clinical practice guidelines (AASLD and NASPGHAN), and authoritative textbook chapters in pediatric gastroenterology and hepatology.

How does the AI cite Dr. Rosenthal's research papers and clinical guidelines?

Every synthesis incorporates verifiable reference tags linked to specific peer-reviewed papers. Responses feature publication titles, co-authors, journals, and publication dates, enabling users to immediately track statements directly back to the original literature on PubMed, institutional libraries, or publisher portals.

The pilot program is accessible to academic medical centers, pediatric hepatology divisions, fellowship programs, and clinical researchers. If you are interested in pilot access, academic evaluation, or institutional integration, you can request credentials through the pilot registration link or submit an inquiry via our contact channel.
