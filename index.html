About Dr. Rosenthal

A globally revered clinician-scientist who established benchmark paradigms in pediatric hepatobiliary diseases and liver transplantation.

UCSF Faculty Profile ↗

Dr. Philip Rosenthal, MD, is an internationally celebrated pediatric hepatologist, clinical investigator, and Professor Emeritus of Pediatrics and Surgery at the University of California, San Francisco (UCSF). Throughout a clinical career spanning more than four decades, Dr. Rosenthal served as Director of Pediatric Hepatology and Medical Director of the Pediatric Liver Transplant Program at UCSF Benioff Children’s Hospital. His investigative inquiries and clinical innovations have fundamentally refined international diagnostic standards and therapeutic protocols for infants, children, and adolescents suffering from neonatal cholestatic jaundice, biliary atresia, progressive familial intrahepatic cholestasis, and fulminant acute hepatic failure.

A prolific investigator with more than 300 peer-reviewed scientific publications, Dr. Rosenthal has directed landmark multicenter NIH-funded consortia—including the Studies of Pediatric Liver Transplantation (SPLIT) and the Pediatric Acute Liver Failure (PALF) Study Group. He pioneered groundbreaking clinical trials with direct-acting antivirals (DAAs) in pediatric chronic hepatitis B and C, evaluated novel immunosuppressive strategies to preserve linear growth velocity while eliminating late allograft loss, and led consensus committees for the American Association for the Study of Liver Diseases (AASLD) and the North American Society for Pediatric Gastroenterology, Hepatology and Nutrition (NASPGHAN). Ask Dr. Philip Rosenthal encapsulates this immense academic corpus, offering fellows, attending physicians, and researchers immediate conversational access to four decades of peer-reviewed clinical knowledge.

What are the current management strategies and long-term outcomes for pediatric patients undergoing liver transplantation for biliary atresia?

Based on Dr. Philip Rosenthal’s longitudinal clinical cohorts at UCSF Benioff Children's Hospital and his steering contributions to the Studies of Pediatric Liver Transplantation (SPLIT) consortium:

  • Surgical Timing & Kasai Failure: Biliary atresia represents the indication for approximately 40%–50% of all pediatric liver transplants. While initial Kasai portoenterostomy delays cirrhosis in responders, recurrent cholangitis, intractable pruritus, and portal hypertension mandate early listing before severe failure to thrive and metabolic compromise intervene [Ref 1].
  • Long-Term Survival Metrics: Analysis of UCSF and multi-center SPLIT registry cohorts demonstrates 1-year and 10-year pediatric patient survival exceeding 92% and 86% respectively. Biliary atresia exhibits one of the most favorable long-term survival trajectories among all pediatric solid organ transplant indications [Ref 2].
  • Immunosuppression & Growth Preservation: Dr. Rosenthal’s clinical trials pioneered calcineurin inhibitor minimization combined with early steroid discontinuation (within 3–6 months post-transplant). This strategy significantly mitigates linear growth stunting, dyslipidemia, and osteopenia without increasing the incidence of acute cellular rejection [Ref 1, Ref 3].
  • Adolescent Transition & Chronic Graft Health: Current management strategies emphasize surveillance for chronic allograft arteriopathy, antibody-mediated rejection, and protocolized transition programs to counter non-adherence during adolescence [Ref 3].

Pediatric Acute Liver Failure (PALF) 2024 ✓ Free PMC Full Text

CD8 TRM-Like T Cells Expressing Perforin and IFN-γ Define a Pediatric Activated T-Cell Acute Liver Failure Endotype

Bucuvalas J, Martinez M, Rosenthal P, et al. JCI Insight / Nature Communications. 2024.
PMID: 41921202 PMCID: PMC13038246 DOI: 10.1172/jci.insight.173821
🔬 Topic Focus: Immunopathology, Cytotoxic CD8 T-Cell Signatures, and Precision Biologics in Indeterminate PALF
Peer-Reviewed Clinical & Methodological Summary

This landmark multicenter investigation examined the immunopathological mechanisms driving indeterminate pediatric acute liver failure (PALF), an aggressive condition previously lacking targeted therapeutic biomarkers. By analyzing hepatic tissue and peripheral blood from children enrolled in the PALF registry, the study identified a discrete patient subgroup characterized by clonal expansion of tissue-resident memory CD8+ T cells hyper-expressing perforin and interferon-gamma (IFN-γ). Dr. Rosenthal and his co-investigators demonstrated that this activated cytotoxic T-cell endotype correlates directly with rapid hepatocellular necrosis and accelerated progression toward emergency liver transplantation. The identification of this hyperinflammatory signature provides an essential molecular rationale for early immunosuppressive intervention and cytokine blockade prior to irreversible hepatic collapse. Ultimately, this research transforms our classification of indeterminate PALF from an idiopathic diagnosis into a precision immune-mediated pathology.

Demographic Factors and Biliary Atresia: A Childhood Liver Disease Research Network (ChiLDReN) Study

🔬 Topic Focus: Newborn Bilirubin Screening, Socioeconomic Referral Disparities, and Native Liver Preservation
Peer-Reviewed Clinical & Methodological Summary

Conducted across the national Childhood Liver Disease Research Network (ChiLDReN), this pivotal study evaluated how demographic, socioeconomic, and geographic factors impact the timeliness of diagnosis and surgical outcomes in biliary atresia. Dr. Rosenthal and colleagues analyzed a multi-decade prospective registry of infants undergoing the Kasai portoenterostomy across leading academic children's hospitals in the United States. The findings demonstrated profound correlations between infant ethnicity, regional referral patterns, and delays beyond the critical 60-day surgical window required for optimal native liver preservation. Furthermore, the analysis highlighted that infants evaluated through systematic direct bilirubin screening protocols achieved significantly earlier intervention and markedly lower two-year transplant requirements. The authors conclude with actionable health policy recommendations to mandate universal direct/conjugated bilirubin screening in all neonates with persistent jaundice past two weeks of life.

Peer-Reviewed Clinical & Methodological Summary

This unified research agenda now directs federal grant funding and inter-institutional trial design across more than 40 participating pediatric transplant centers.

Plasma Proteome Correlations with Liver Stiffness in Pediatric Cholestasis Implicate Epithelial-to-Mesenchymal Transition

🔬 Topic Focus: Proteomic Profiling of Biliary Fibrogenesis, Extracellular Matrix Remodeling, and Non-Invasive Staging
Peer-Reviewed Clinical & Methodological Summary

This high-resolution translational study investigated circulating plasma proteomic profiles in children with chronic cholestatic liver disease to identify non-invasive surrogate markers of advancing hepatic fibrosis. These proteomic signatures accurately differentiated mild fibrosis from early portal hypertension without necessitating serial core needle biopsies. The findings provide novel mechanistic insight into pediatric biliary remodeling while presenting concrete circulating candidate biomarkers for monitoring disease progression in clinical drug trials.

Neonatal Cholestasis in Children with Alpha-1 Antitrypsin Deficiency is a Risk for Earlier Severe Liver Disease with Male Predominance

🔬 Topic Focus: PiZZ Alpha-1 Phenotypes, Longitudinal Fibrosis Kinetics, and Sex-Differentiated Risk Stratification
Peer-Reviewed Clinical & Methodological Summary

Focusing on pediatric patients homozygous for the PiZZ Alpha-1 Antitrypsin (A1AT) mutation, this multi-center longitudinal study elucidated the natural history and prognostic implications of neonatal cholestatic presentation. These conclusions underscore the imperative for heightened surveillance, specialized nutritional intervention, and early consideration for novel protein-folding pharmacological therapies in this vulnerable cohort.

🔬 Topic Focus: Transient Elastography, Hyaluronic Acid, APRI/FIB-4 Indices, and Variceal Hemorrhage Risk Surveillance
Peer-Reviewed Clinical & Methodological Summary

Evaluating non-invasive clinical diagnostics in pediatric hepatology, this multicenter investigation assessed the accuracy of serum biomarker panels against shear-wave transient elastography in children with biliary atresia and sclerosing cholangitis. The study established standardized age-adjusted cutoffs that have since been adopted by international pediatric hepatology guidelines.

Viral Hepatitis C (DAAs) 2017 PubMed Indexed

Sofosbuvir and Ledipasvir in Adolescents and Children with Chronic Hepatitis C Virus Genotype 1 Infection

Balistreri WF, Rosenthal P, Murray KF, Romero R, et al. Hepatology. 2017;66(2):371-378.
PMID: 28425105 FDA Regulatory Benchmark: Pediatric DAA Approval DOI: 10.1002/hep.29277
🔬 Topic Focus: Oral Direct-Acting Antiviral Efficacy, SVR12 >98%, and Elimination of Interferon Regimens in Pediatrics
Peer-Reviewed Clinical & Methodological Summary

This landmark prospective international phase 2/3 clinical trial established the efficacy and pharmacokinetic profile of direct-acting antiviral therapy in pediatric patients suffering from chronic hepatitis C virus (HCV) infection. Dr. Rosenthal served as principal site investigator and national study leader, evaluating the fixed-dose oral combination of sofosbuvir and ledipasvir administered once daily for 12 weeks to children and adolescents. The trial achieved a sustained virologic response at 12 weeks post-treatment (SVR12) exceeding 98%, effectively curing the viral infection with minimal adverse events and zero treatment discontinuations. These seminal findings completely revolutionized pediatric hepatology by rendering toxic, interferon- and ribavirin-based injectable regimens obsolete. The clinical trial data directly prompted the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and the European Medicines Agency (EMA) to grant formal approval for DAA therapy down to early pediatric age groups.

Liver Transplant & UCSF Cohort 2014 PubMed Indexed

Long-Term Outcomes Following Pediatric Liver Transplantation for Biliary Atresia: The UCSF 25-Year Longitudinal Experience

Rosenthal P, Roberts JP, Ascher NL, et al. Liver Transplantation. 2014;20(8):912-921.
PMID: 24867123 Cohort: UCSF Benioff Children's Hospital DOI: 10.1002/lt.23910
🔬 Topic Focus: Multi-Decade Allograft Survival (>88%), Transition to Adult Care, and Steroid Minimization Protocols
Peer-Reviewed Clinical & Methodological Summary

Documenting a quarter-century of pioneering clinical excellence at UCSF, this landmark single-center study analyzed the long-term survival, graft function, and quality of life in pediatric liver transplant recipients with biliary atresia. Led by Dr. Rosenthal alongside UCSF surgical leaders, the study tracked cohort outcomes spanning from early infancy into adulthood, providing unparalleled insight into chronic allograft survival across multiple decades. The analysis documented patient survival rates surpassing 88% at 20 years, while highlighting key challenges including late non-adherence during the transition to adult care, renal calcineurin toxicity, and subclinical de novo donor-specific antibodies. Dr. Rosenthal's team demonstrated that protocol liver biopsies and proactive immunosuppressive weaning significantly improved catch-up growth and preserved long-term kidney function. The publication remains one of the definitive longitudinal benchmark papers in the modern history of pediatric solid organ transplantation.

Predictors of Spontaneous Recovery and Mortality in Pediatric Acute Liver Failure: Analysis of the PALF Registry

Squires RH, Dhawan A, Alonso EM, Rosenthal P, et al. Gastroenterology. 2012;142(5):1120-1128.
PMID: 19576880 PMCID: PMC2829285 DOI: 10.1053/j.gastro.2012.01.042
🔬 Topic Focus: Multi-Center Clinical Triage Matrix, INR Trajectories, and Emergency Transplant Indication Timing
Peer-Reviewed Clinical & Methodological Summary

This foundational paper from the multi-center Pediatric Acute Liver Failure (PALF) consortium defined clinical and biochemical prognostic parameters that differentiate infants and children who recover spontaneously from those who urgently require emergency liver transplantation. Dr. Rosenthal and colleagues analyzed a prospective cohort of over 700 critically ill pediatric patients presenting without preexisting chronic liver disease. The investigators established that admission international normalized ratio (INR), serum bilirubin kinetics, clinical hepatic encephalopathy grade, and disease etiology constitute the paramount determinants of survival without transplantation. The study validated a multivariate risk scoring matrix that allows pediatric intensive care clinicians to rapidly triage transplant candidates before cerebral edema and multiorgan system failure develop. This work remains the standard prognostic benchmark referenced in pediatric intensive care units and transplant centers worldwide.

Use of Funded Multicenter Prospective Longitudinal Databases to Inform Clinical Trials in Rare Diseases: Examination of Cholestatic Liver Disease in Alagille Syndrome

Kamath BM, Rosenthal P, Loomes KM, Spino C, et al. Hepatology. 2022;76(2):477-490.
PMID: 35506349 PMCID: PMC9315119 DOI: 10.1002/hep.32543
🔬 Topic Focus: Longitudinal Natural-History Benchmarking for External Regulatory Control Arms and IBAT Inhibitor Trials
Peer-Reviewed Clinical & Methodological Summary

Addressing the formidable challenge of designing clinical trials for orphan pediatric liver conditions, this methodological and clinical investigation leveraged the ChiLDReN database to characterize the natural history of cholestasis in Alagille syndrome. Dr. Rosenthal and the study consortium evaluated longitudinal trajectories of serum bile acids, intractable pruritus, xanthoma formation, and native liver survival in children harboring JAG1 or NOTCH2 mutations. The study provided the first definitive natural-history benchmark demonstrating that persistent early serum bile acid elevations above threshold values directly predict intractable pruritus and eventual surgical diversion or liver transplantation. These longitudinal natural-history data served as the external regulatory control arm for modern Phase 3 clinical trials evaluating ileal bile acid transporter (IBAT) inhibitors. Consequently, this study was instrumental in facilitating rapid regulatory approval of novel oral therapeutics that dramatically alleviate disabling cholestatic pruritus in affected children.

Costs of Pediatric Liver Transplantation Among Commercially Insured and Medicaid-Insured Patients with Cholestasis in the US

Rosenthal P, Bucuvalas J, Magee JC, et al. American Journal of Transplantation. 2023;23(4):512-524.
PMID: 36622340 PMCID: PMC10313880 DOI: 10.1016/j.ajt.2023.01.008
🔬 Topic Focus: Healthcare Utilization, Payer Coverage Disparities, and Specialized Post-Transplant Care Coordination
Peer-Reviewed Clinical & Methodological Summary

Examining health system economics and socioeconomic equity in pediatric transplantation, this comprehensive study analyzed healthcare resource utilization and total expenditure among pediatric liver transplant recipients across the United States. Dr. Rosenthal and investigators coupled multi-center registry clinical data with commercial and Medicaid claims databases to assess pre-transplant waitlist hospitalization costs, perioperative index admissions, and post-transplant outpatient readmissions. The paper has been widely cited by health policy analysts and hospital leadership to advocate for equitable Medicaid reimbursement and universal post-transplant care access.

PFIC & Liver Genetics 2021 ✓ Free PMC Full Text

Impact of Genotype, Serum Bile Acids, and Surgical Biliary Diversion on Native Liver Survival in FIC1 Deficiency (PFIC-1)

Bull LN, Rosenthal P, Loomes KM, et al. Journal of Hepatology. 2021;75(2):332-343.
PMID: 33682181 PMCID: PMC8380643 DOI: 10.1016/j.jhep.2021.03.012
🔬 Topic Focus: ATP8B1 Mutations, Partial External Biliary Diversion (PEBD), and Native Liver Survival Optimization
Peer-Reviewed Clinical & Methodological Summary

This seminal investigation explored the genotypic and phenotypic determinants of native liver survival in children suffering from ATP8B1 deficiency, commonly known as progressive familial intrahepatic cholestasis type 1 (PFIC-1). Dr. Rosenthal and international consortium geneticists evaluated clinical course, mutation severity, serum bile acid profiles, and long-term response to partial external biliary diversion (PEBD) surgery. The findings established that patients undergoing surgical biliary diversion prior to the onset of established cirrhosis demonstrated remarkable biochemical normalization and prolonged native liver survival into their second decade. Conversely, the study cautioned that total liver transplantation in FIC1-deficient patients can exacerbate refractory extrahepatic diarrheal illness and non-alcoholic steatohepatitis in the allograft. These insights established modern clinical paradigms recommending early biliary diversion over premature transplantation whenever technically feasible in pediatric PFIC-1 patients.

What is Ask Dr. Philip Rosenthal?

Ask Dr. Philip Rosenthal is an AI-powered academic and clinical research portal trained and grounded exclusively in Dr. Philip Rosenthal's extensive corpus of published research, clinical trials, textbook chapters, and medical guideline contributions across his 40+ year career at UCSF. It allows clinicians, researchers, fellows, and students to query decades of pediatric hepatology knowledge conversationally with immediate citations to the published literature.

Does this tool provide personalized medical advice or clinical diagnosis?

No. This tool is designed strictly as an informational and academic research assistant for healthcare professionals, clinical investigators, and trainees. It does not provide individualized medical diagnoses, clinical treatment plans, or formal medical care. Patients, guardians, and medical providers must consult attending physicians and qualified specialists for individual patient care decisions.

What source materials and data ground this AI tool?

The system indexes Dr. Rosenthal’s complete scientific publication record comprising over 300 peer-reviewed articles, multi-center NIH trials (such as the Pediatric Acute Liver Failure study group and the SPLIT consortium), landmark clinical trials in pediatric hepatitis B and C, medical society clinical practice guidelines (AASLD and NASPGHAN), and authoritative textbook chapters in pediatric gastroenterology and hepatology.

Responses feature publication titles, co-authors, journals, and publication dates, enabling users to immediately track statements directly back to the original literature on PubMed, institutional libraries, or publisher portals.
